## How to post — step by step (the mechanics)

### Composing a tweet

1. Click **"What's happening?"** at the top of your Home timeline (or the "+" floating button on mobile).
2. Type your text — **280 character limit** (free tier). X Premium raises it, but stick with 280 as the discipline forces clarity.
3. Character counter appears in the bottom-right. When you hit 260, tighten. When you hit 280, split.
4. Click **Post**. That's it for a single tweet.

### Composing a thread (multi-tweet post)

1. Write your first tweet. **Do not post yet.**
2. Click the **"+"** below the text box → adds a second tweet composer.
3. Repeat for each tweet. Number them **1/3, 2/3, 3/3** so readers know when it ends.
4. Click **Post all** when done. All tweets publish as a linked thread.

Keep threads to **3 tweets max.** Longer belongs on LinkedIn.

### Attaching images / screenshots

1. Click the **image icon** below the text box (or drag-drop an image onto the composer).
2. Up to **4 images per tweet.** For screenshots, 1 image is enough — 2+ splits attention.
3. **Add ALT text** on every image (click "Add description"). Accessibility + screen-reader visibility + the ALT text is indexed by search.
4. Ideal aspect ratio for screenshots: **16:9** or **1200×675 px.** Anything narrower gets cropped in the timeline preview.

### Quote-tweeting (adds your take to someone else's post)

1. Click the **Retweet icon** (two arrows) below the target tweet.
2. Choose **"Quote"** instead of "Repost".
3. Write your take (up to 280 chars) — usually a specific technical angle, not "great post!"
4. Post.

Quote-tweets give you access to the original poster's audience. Best used on OPC Foundation, Ignition, HighByte, Siemens announcements.

### Replying (to your own tweet or someone else's)

1. Click the **reply icon** (speech bubble) below the target tweet.
2. Type your reply — 280 chars.
3. Post.

**Replying to your own tweet** = building a thread after the fact. Useful for follow-ups ("Update — here's what changed 3 days later ↓").

### Pinning a tweet

1. Open the tweet you want pinned (e.g. your anchor).
2. Click the **three dots** in the top-right of the tweet.
3. Choose **"Pin to your profile."** Only one tweet can be pinned at a time.

Pin your anchor tweet (Post 1 below). Refresh it every 4 weeks with the latest milestone.

### Posting in an X Community

1. Left sidebar → **"Communities"** → search + join 3–5 (see list below).
2. When posting, X asks: post to your timeline OR to the Community.
3. Choose the Community when the topic fits (never the anchor tweet — that goes on your timeline).
4. **Max 1 community post per week per community.** More = shadowban.

## Cadence — 3 posts / week

**Content mix (updated 2026-07-05):** every post follows a **problem → solution** shape. Concrete plant pain first, then what MindSet does about it, then the beta CTA. No abstract vision-only posts, no pure founder-journey posts, no stack details.

You're still exploring the technical stack — so *how* the solution works stays vague ("live on the shop floor", "one click", "one live view"). *What* the solution does can be specific ("catches sub-minute stops", "brings SCADA + ERP + LIMS into one view"). The distinction is what protects you if the internals pivot.

Safe territory:
- **Problem side:** plant-manager quotes, factory-visit observations, gap between what tools promise and what shifts actually get
- **Solution side:** capability claims at the level a Directeur d'Usine cares about — real-time, on the box, in one click, before shift ends

Off-limits: named libraries, protocol version numbers, architectural patterns, ISA-95 heuristics, engine details, database choice.

| Day | Type |
|---|---|
| **Tuesday** | Problem story — a real (or realistic) plant scene, ending with what MindSet does about it |
| **Wednesday or Thursday** | Contrast post — "current tools do X, MindSet does Y" — quantified when possible |
| **Friday** | Screenshot / product moment OR milestone quote-tweet — solution in action |

Weekends: no posts. Reply to comments only.

**Guardrail:** if a draft names a specific tool/library/protocol depth, cut it. If it's pure vision with no plant reality, cut it. Every tweet earns its place by naming a real problem AND what your product does about it.

## Anti-patterns — NEVER do these

- ❌ **Engagement pods** (private DM groups that trade likes/retweets) — X actively shadowbans
- ❌ **Mass DMs** to strangers pitching the beta — spam classifier will nuke you
- ❌ **Buying followers** — kills credibility, obvious to anyone who checks
- ❌ **Auto-scheduling identical tweets** across days — pattern-repetition triggers spam filter
- ❌ **Posting the same tweet in 5 Communities the same day** — shadowban risk
- ❌ **Bad-mouthing competitors** by name — subtle differentiation YES, direct attack NEVER
- ❌ **Politics, religion, hot-take culture wars** — kills B2B credibility instantly
- ❌ **"Buy MindSet" pitches** — the beta link belongs at the end, contextual, once per post
- ❌ **Cross-posting your LinkedIn content unchanged** — LinkedIn tone kills on X
