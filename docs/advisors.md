## Operating principles (apply to both advisors)

### 1. Sovereignty alignment is non-negotiable

**Status**: NOT YET COMMUNICATED. Schedule the 15-min alignment conversation with each before the next regular call. See `analysis_log.md` Entry 27 for the script + 4 pushback patterns with one-liner responses.

Send them BEFORE the conversation:
- `docs/memo_cecilia_FR.md` (the French 1-pager)
- Sheets 1, 3, 4 of `MindSet_Competitive_Analysis_v2_3.xlsx` (Positioning, 5 Moats, 3 Editions)
- `docs/decisions.md` (the canonical 25+ decisions log)

So they walk in informed, not blindsided.

### 2. WhatsApp discipline

WhatsApp = **high-priority interrupts only.** Specifically:
- "Investor X just asked us Y — what's your immediate read?" (pre-meeting prep)
- "We have an enterprise customer interested in Z — do you know anyone there?" (warm intro)
- "Question from a developer candidate about the role — quick reaction?" (hiring loop)

All non-urgent → goes into the weekly call brief.

**Target: 2-3 WhatsApp pings per week per advisor. Not 20.** Abuse the channel → they'll mute you. Under-use → you waste the asymmetric advantage.

### 3. Weekly call protocol

- **24h before the call**: email a 1-page brief with 3 specific questions / asks for that call. No vague status updates.
- **In the call**: focused on the 3 questions. Don't drift into general status.
- **End every call with ONE specific ask** they can do before next week. Concrete + due-dated.
- **Track in this doc**: what was asked, when, what was delivered.

### 3b. How to make an ask that actually delivers (the template)

The mistake most CTOs make: vague asks ("can you help with sales?"). Vague asks get vague responses and waste the advisor's hour.

**Every ask must have all 4 components:**

> 1. **Specific outcome wanted**
> 2. **Concrete artifact to act on** (a 1-pager, a 4-option list, a candidate brief)
> 3. **Due date**
> 4. **Definition of "good"**

**Bad ask**: *"Jalil, can you help us with hiring?"*

**Good ask**:
> *"Jalil, we're hiring our first engineer. Profile: Senior Go + React, EU-based, French-speaking, comfortable with industrial/IoT. Comp: competitive salary + up to 2% equity. Attached is the 1-page brief. ASK: forward this to 3 candidates from your Google network by next Friday. Good outcome: 3 candidates agree to a 30-min screen with us within 2 weeks."*

**Bad ask**: *"Djamil, what do you think about our pricing?"*

**Good ask**:
> *"Djamil, we have 4 pricing options on the table (attached 1-pager). ASK: spend 30 minutes by next Friday — react to each option (gut feel + concerns + which best fits EU sovereign mid-market), and recommend which to anchor on for the investor pitch. Good outcome: a clear recommendation + 3-5 reasons."*

**Always send the artifact 24h before the call.** No artifact = no productive call.

### 3c. Specialist Booster brokering — exact script

When you need a specialist (Mehdi for AI review, Edmond for legal, etc.), broker through Jalil or Djamil — **don't cold-message the specialist directly**.

> *"[Jalil / Djamil] — we hit a specific question this week: [the question, 1-2 sentences].
> Based on the Boost10x team, [Specialist Name] looks like the ideal person to react to it. ASK: can you broker a 30-min intro?
> If yes, here's a 1-pager I'd send them in advance: [link]. Their time, our agenda, your relationship as the bridge."*

Why this protocol:
- **Protects the Chief Booster relationship** — they control specialist access, important for trust within Boost10x
- **Makes the specialist consultation high-signal** — the specialist arrives briefed, not blindsided
- **Keeps Jalil/Djamil informed** of which specialists you're tapping → they can warn you if a particular specialist is over-booked or out of scope
